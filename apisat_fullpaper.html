Knowledge-Injected Reinforcement Learning for VRS-Safe UAM Landing:
Human Marking, Curriculum Training, and PINN Dynamics

Jaerim Kim1 and Sungwook Cho2
1Department of Mechanical and Aeronautical Systems Engineering, Cheongju University, Cheongju, Chungbuk, Republic of Korea
2Department of Aeronautical and Mechanical Engineering, Cheongju University, Cheongju, Chungbuk, Republic of Korea
Abstract Safe autonomous landing of eVTOL aircraft in urban environments poses a critical challenge for Urban Air Mobility (UAM). Rotorcraft descending through rotor-induced wake enter Vortex Ring State (VRS)—causing sudden lift loss that can result in control failure. Existing RL methods depend solely on reward shaping, which restricts sample efficiency and prevents early detection of gradual velocity drift toward dangerous regimes. We propose a knowledge injection framework combining three mechanisms: human-curated state marking via a Context Database (Context DB), ontology-based state classification, and Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) virtual experience generation. Validated on CartPole Swing-up, the framework raises mean balance reward from 195.8 to 323.4 (+65%), lifts 180° swing-up success from 11.3% to 59.3% through curriculum learning, and achieves 80.0% at 180° after PINN+Context fine-tuning—versus 0% for SAC-only and PINN+SAC without marking.

1. Introduction

Urban Air Mobility (UAM) requires safe autonomous landing of electric Vertical Take-Off and Landing (eVTOL) aircraft in complex urban environments. A critical hazard arises during descent: rotorcraft entering their own rotor-induced wake can experience Vortex Ring State (VRS), a flight condition characterized by sudden and severe loss of thrust that can result in catastrophic control failure [1,2]. Building-induced turbulence and residual rotor wake at urban vertiports further amplify this risk [3].

Conventional reinforcement learning (RL) approaches address VRS avoidance primarily through reward shaping—assigning penalties for VRS entry. This strategy faces two fundamental limitations. First, RL generates probabilistic policies that require extensive exploration to identify safe flight corridors. Second, sparse and delayed rewards prevent the agent from detecting gradual velocity drift toward dangerous flight regimes before VRS entry occurs [4].

To overcome these limitations, we propose a knowledge injection framework that enriches RL training by incorporating three complementary knowledge sources without modifying the underlying learning algorithm: (1) human-curated state annotations via a Context Database (Context DB), (2) ontology-based state classification enabling structured safety-regime reasoning, and (3) Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) virtual experience generation that augments training with physically consistent transitions.

Because UAM VRS simulation infrastructure is under active development, we first validate the framework on CartPole Swing-up—a benchmark that mirrors key challenges of the target problem: nonlinear dynamics, sparse rewards, and staged controller transitions. This enables rigorous component-level verification before UAM deployment.

2.1 Context Database and Human Marking

The Context DB stores human-annotated critical states identified during live simulation. A human operator marked 57 key states across CartPole and UAM domains—37 for balance training and 22 for swing-up switching-point transitions. During training, when the agent visits a state within distance ε of a marked state, it receives a Context DB bonus reward, providing dense guidance where task rewards are otherwise sparse.

2.2 Ontology-Based State Classification

States are classified into three regimes—Normal, Caution, and Danger—using an ontology-based classifier. This structured labeling gives the agent qualitatively differentiated safety signals analogous to real avionics warning systems. In CartPole, Caution maps to high angular velocity near the balance threshold; in UAM, it maps to the VRS onset band (Vz/vh ∈ [0.5, 1.0]).

2.3 Physics-Informed Neural Network

The PINN dynamics model is trained with a composite loss:

ℒ = ℒdata + λphys · ℒphysics

where ℒdata is the mean squared prediction error over observed transitions and ℒphysics is the Lagrangian residual derived from the equations of motion. This dual supervision ensures learned dynamics remain physically consistent in sparse-data regions. Prediction errors of 0.001–0.005 were achieved. PINN-generated virtual transitions augment the real replay buffer in a 1:1 ratio, effectively doubling sample efficiency.

2.4 Curriculum Learning with Staged Switching

Swing-up employs a two-stage architecture: SAC handles energy injection during swing-up, and PPO takes over stabilization once |θ| < 20°. The switching point is precisely where Context DB marking is most critical. Curriculum learning progresses through four angle stages (45°→90°→135°→180°), with each stage inheriting the best policy checkpoint of the prior stage and 22 human-marked switching-point states injected at each transition.

3. CartPole Validation

3.1 Basic Balance: Human Marking Effect

We first validated the Context DB bonus mechanism on standard CartPole-v1 balance using PPO. A human operator marked 37 critical states during live simulation—primarily pole angles near ±12° where the optimal action is non-obvious. These states were stored as Context DB entries with ε = 0.3 distance threshold and bonus reward of +2.0.

Context DB augmentation raised mean episode reward from 195.8 to 323.4 (+65%) and achieved a 2.5× faster convergence rate. This confirmed that human-curated dense guidance effectively addresses reward sparsity in critical regions (Fig. 2a).

3.2 Swing-Up Curriculum: Marking Effect

Swing-up success was evaluated by measuring the switching success rate—the fraction of episodes in which SAC successfully transfers control to PPO in the near-vertical region. Curriculum training without marking achieved only 11.3% at the 180° stage, reflecting the difficulty of the final stage after four sequential transfers.

Injecting 22 human-marked switching-point states raised the 180° success rate to 59.3%—a 5.2× improvement. Marked states provided the agent with concentrated experience at the switching boundary, where the policy must transition from energy-maximizing swing-up to stabilization (Fig. 2b).

PINN+SAC substantially outperforms the baseline across early stages, with particularly large gains at 90° (99.3% vs. 43.7%). However, at 180°—the hardest stage—both SAC-only and PINN+SAC without Context DB marking achieved 0%. Only the combined PINN+Context+SAC fine-tuning reached 80.0%, a 20.7 percentage-point gain over the pre-fine-tuning checkpoint (59.3%). This confirms the decisive role of Human-in-the-Loop marking: physics-augmented RL alone cannot solve the final stage without human-curated guidance at the switching boundary.

4. UAM VRS Extension

Ongoing work extends the validated framework to VRS-safe UAM landing. A custom low-fidelity VRS simulation environment is implemented based on the momentum-theory envelope, with Glauert polynomial correction in the transition band (Vz/vh ∈ [0.5, 2.0]) and RK45 numerical integration (dt = 0.0025 s). The SAC policy controls descent velocity Vz and horizontal velocity Vx.

Preliminary curriculum training results on Stage 1 (altitude 20–40 m) yield a 9.5% landing success rate using the redesigned reward structure with no survival bonus—a first successful landing achievement. Stage 2 (60–90 m) is currently under evaluation. Initial results are targeted for the conference presentation. Performance will be assessed by VRS entry rate, envelope violation count, landing success rate, and time efficiency against PID and baseline SAC controllers.

5. Conclusion

We present a knowledge injection framework for RL-based safe landing control that combines human-curated Context DB marking, ontology-based state classification, and PINN virtual experience generation. Validated on CartPole Swing-up, the framework demonstrates consistent improvements across all three components: human marking raises balance reward by 65% and lifts swing-up success 5.2×; PINN augmentation provides large gains in early curriculum stages; and the combined PINN+Context+SAC fine-tuning uniquely solves the hardest 180° stage at 80.0%—confirming that human-in-the-loop knowledge is indispensable where reward signals are most sparse.

These results establish the component-level validity of each mechanism before UAM deployment. Extension to the full VRS-safe landing task is underway, with initial results to be reported at the conference.
